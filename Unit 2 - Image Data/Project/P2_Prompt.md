### Choosing your classes

**You choose your own categories**, subject to these constraints:

- **3–10 classes.** Fewer than 3 makes the confusion matrix uninteresting. More than 10 makes the scraping and cleaning workload unreasonable for the time available.
- **At least 200 usable images per class *after* cleaning.** That is a floor on what survives Notebook 2, not on what you download — so scrape well beyond 200. Expect to lose a meaningful fraction to junk and duplicates; scraping 300–400 per class to land 200 is normal. If a class cannot reach 200, pick a different class — do not pad it with junk.
- **Not a standard benchmark.** No cats-vs-dogs, no MNIST digits, no CIFAR categories. The point is a dataset that has never been curated by anyone.
- **Visually distinguishable but not trivial.** Classes that any model separates at 99% teach you nothing, and neither do classes that are genuinely ambiguous. Aim for categories a person could sort reliably but not instantly — different tools, dog breeds, architectural styles, food dishes, plant species, guitar body shapes, cloud types.

> **On the images themselves.** Scraped images are copyrighted by whoever made them. They are fine to use for coursework under fair use, but do **NOT** redistribute them, do **NOT** commit them to your GitHub repo beyond the one sample per class, and do **NOT** publish your dataset anywhere.

## Notebook 3 — Transfer Learning
*Filename: `P2_Images-3_TransferLearning_<TeamName>.ipynb`*

Read in the cleaned images from Notebook 2. Use clear markdown section headers to delineate each stage and each model. The central question of this notebook is **how much does model size actually buy you on a small custom dataset?**

### Pipeline order

Run the data pipeline in **exactly this order**, and explain in a markdown cell why each step sits where it does:

1. **Train/test split** — stratified. Split *first*, before anything else touches the data. Balancing or augmenting before the split leaks synthetic or duplicated copies of training images into your test set, and your accuracy becomes fiction
2. **Balance** — the **training set only**. Your test set stays at its natural class distribution, because that is what you are trying to measure. Oversample the minority classes, or use class weights — justify your choice against `U2-2_CNN-3_Imbalanced`
3. **Augment** — the **training set only**. Define your transforms and justify each **for your subject matter**: horizontal flips are free for most objects and wrong for anything with text or handedness; heavy rotation is right for photographed-from-above categories and wrong for buildings. Show a grid of one image under several random augmentations
4. **Embed** — push the balanced, augmented training set and the untouched test set through each frozen backbone to get feature vectors

Embedding **last** is what makes the rest of the notebook affordable: a frozen backbone gives the same output for the same image every time, so you pay for the forward pass once and then train heads on feature vectors in seconds. Note the consequence — each augmented copy is a distinct image and gets its own embedding, so augment before you embed, not after.

### Models

Your submission **MUST** include:

- **A from-scratch baseline**
    - Train a small CNN from random initialization on your data — a few conv blocks, as in `U2-2_CNN-4_Cifar`
    - This is the number every pretrained model has to beat, and it establishes how hard your task actually is
- **AT LEAST THREE pretrained backbones spanning a real size range** — small, medium, and large. From `keras.applications`, reasonable picks are:

    | Tier | Example | Approx. params |
    |---|---|---:|
    | Small | `MobileNetV2`, `EfficientNetV2B0` | 3–7 M |
    | Medium | `ResNet50`, `EfficientNetV2S` | 21–25 M |
    | Large | `EfficientNetV2M`, `ConvNeXtBase` | 54–88 M |

    - Load each with `include_top=False`, freeze it, and train a classification head, following `U2-2_CNN-6_TransferLearning`
    - **Apply each backbone's own `preprocess_input`.** This is the single most common way this assignment goes wrong: MobileNetV2 wants $[-1,1]$, EfficientNetV2 has rescaling baked in and wants raw 0–255. Feeding a model the wrong scaling produces a quietly mediocre result and no error message. State in a markdown cell what each of your backbones expects
- **Fine-tuning**
    - Take **AT LEAST ONE** backbone further: after its head has converged, unfreeze the top block(s) and continue training at a much lower learning rate (~1e-5)
    - Report the before/after and discuss whether it was worth the extra compute
    - Order matters — fine-tuning before the head converges destroys the pretrained weights you came for
    - Note that a fine-tuned backbone is no longer frozen, so its precomputed embeddings are stale and must be recomputed
- **Evaluation** — for **EVERY** model above:
    - Accuracy, macro-F1, a full classification report, and a confusion matrix with **class names on the axes**
    - Per-class recall — an average hides a class the model has given up on, which matters more the more classes you chose
- **Comparison**
    - A single summary table: model, parameter count, trainable parameter count, training time, test accuracy, macro-F1
    - A plot of **accuracy vs. parameter count**
    - Discuss: does bigger win? Where do the returns flatten? Was the largest model worth its training time on a dataset this size? What did pretraining buy over the from-scratch baseline?
    - Revisit your Notebook 1 prediction: was the pair you expected to be confused actually the pair the confusion matrix confuses?
- **Export**
    - Save every trained model to `models/` as `P2_N<num>_<backbone>_<variant>.keras` — e.g. `P2_N3_mobilenetv2_frozen.keras`, `P2_N3_effnetv2m_finetuned.keras`, `P2_N3_scratch_cnn.keras`
    - Save the label→index mapping next to them

> If your test accuracy is at or near 100%, be suspicious before you celebrate. On a small scraped dataset that usually means duplicate leakage across splits or classes separated by a background artifact rather than the subject. Go back to your Notebook 2 dedup check, and let Notebook 4 tell you what the model is really using.

## Notebook 4 — Interpretability (Saliency & CAM)
